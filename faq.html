Questions

Plain answers, including what is real and what is not.

Is this real money?

No. Inkwell runs on NEAR testnet, a practice network. The coins have no value, and nobody pays or is paid in real money.

What is real, and what is simulated?

Real on testnet: the escrow contract, deposits, payouts, the profit split, the restricted key, and the blockchain’s refusals. Simulated: the token a customer says they pay with (NEAR Intents has no testnet), the bidders’ prices, and the “Run the studio” console, which runs in your browser and not on-chain.

What stops the manager from overspending?

Its key can call one function, release, on the escrow contract, and the contract refuses any payout above its cap. The studio also checks every payee and applies daily and per-task limits before it asks.

What does “private” mean here?

Drafts are written by a model that runs in a sealed hardware environment. For each order we fetch an attestation with a fresh random value and check that it comes back with that value and a hardware quote. We do not verify the quote’s signature ourselves; NEAR’s verification page does that deeper check.

How do I place a real order?

Open the order page, type a topic, pick a price from 30 to 60 units and press “Place order on testnet”. A reply with transaction links and your draft appears on the page in about a minute. No account is needed. Each visitor can place 3 orders per hour.

Why did my order get rejected?

The usual reasons are a price outside the range, a second order within an hour, or the shared demo account running low on test coins.

Can I check the transactions myself?

Yes. Every transaction link opens the public NEAR testnet explorer. The proof page lists a full recorded run.

Is it on mainnet?

Not yet. The escrow contract has not been audited, so it only handles test coins.
